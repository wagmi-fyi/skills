# The Method — verifying orchestration

How an orchestration is *run*. Durable and domain-agnostic. Project specifics live in the generated `plan.md`.

## The model
- **Orchestrator** — one persistent, lean session. Owns the unit dependency graph, authors delegated prompts, and **verifies every result by re-deriving it** (read-only). Holds the workpaper. The only thing that decides a unit is "done." Mutates nothing itself; never compiles/builds/deploys — that is session work. Reads structured summaries + reported check output, never the raw work.
- **Working (delegated) sessions** — fresh sessions, one per unit, executing from a context-less prompt and reporting a structured, verifiable summary. They hold the heavy context the orchestrator deliberately does not. Whenever they do **real work**, they are **human-reachable** — a session the human can open, watch and take over — never hidden subagents.
- **Human** — gates **design crossroads** + **human-only / irreversible** steps (see `human-in-the-loop.md`). **Not the message relay** — the bus is.

> Read-only research/verification may run as the orchestrator's own headless subagents — that's not "real work" (no mutations, no deliverables). Anything that writes state or produces a deliverable runs in a reachable session.

## Principles
- **Elicit to ~95% before committing to a design.** Before standing up a plan/spec — or authoring a unit's prompt — elicit until you're ~95% confident you understand the user's intent. The north-star prompt *"elicit until you're 95% confident you understand the user's intent"* is usually enough to surface the requisite detail; restate the goal, definition of done, and constraints back, and ask follow-ups until it's clear. Don't design against a half-understood goal.
- **Requests are approximate; walls are information.** The human's directions are *pointers* toward what they actually want — the simplest, cleanest design that serves the stated goal — and may be slightly off; that goal always outranks their literal words. So when a unit hits a wall (a case that doesn't fit, a spec that breaks, an assumption that fails), the wall is evidence the design is wrong somewhere: **STOP and re-derive from first principles until the wall doesn't exist** — and if the re-derived design diverges from the spec/ruling, presenting the divergence is a *duty*, not insubordination (route it as a crossroads). What is never acceptable is patching around the wall to comply with the words — a flag, a special case, a conversion shim, a second channel, a parallel path, a test rewritten to dodge a broken rule. **The patch is itself the failure**: it betrays intent while pretending to honor it, and it gets rejected regardless of sunk cost. A blocker honestly reported is a good outcome; a "working" deliverable built on a workaround is the worst one.
- **Verify, don't trust.** A reported result is a *hypothesis*. Re-derive every load-bearing fact from source-of-truth before accepting it. Nothing is "done" on a summary's say-so.
- **Respect the graph and the gates.** A dependent unit doesn't start before its upstream is **verified** (not merely "reported").
- **Parallelize cognition, serialize commitment.** Builds/research parallelize freely (disjoint lanes). **Commitment** — the deploy, the publish, the system-of-record write — serializes: one at a time, validated before the next. Across concurrent orchestrations, serialize via a **named lease** (`bus lock` — see `git.md`).
- **Exclusive lanes; reserved commitment surfaces.** This is medium-agnostic — code, books, content, data alike. Every concurrent delegate works a **private, disjoint surface** (a git worktree, a directory, a file set, a schema — whatever the project's medium); two lanes never share a mutable surface. And the **commitment step itself** (the merge, the publish, the SoR write) runs on a surface **reserved for that one operation** — never on a shared everyday surface another session may be mid-flight on. (The git instances — worktree-per-lane, worktree-per-merge — are in `git.md`; apply the same shape in any other medium.)
- **Adapt the fan-out.** Delegate heavy, independent units; handle trivial/tightly-serial ones inline (but anything mutating real state is still a reachable session, never a hidden subagent).
- **Fire human-only asks early.** Their turnaround is usually the real critical path.
- **Gated means a named gate.** When work queues behind an external event, name the *specific* thing it needs — an input (a document, a dataset, a base revision), a scarce resource (a build box, a ledger), a commitment slot (a lease), or a human decision. If no specific gate can be named, the work is parked out of caution — un-park it. Corollary: when a verification's resource is free, run it *now*; early gates are where assumptions meet reality while fixes are still cheap. When unsure whether a peer's milestone gates you, ask them rather than assume.
- **A standing arrangement with a peer survives only on their LIVE surface.** An item handed to a peer orchestration (a queued unit, a promised landing, a manifest entry) exists only as long as it sits on the peer's *live* queue — compactions, crashes, and condenses on either side silently drop handed-off items while both sides' *records* of the handoff stay intact. Re-confirm standing arrangements against the peer's live surface (a one-line manifest check costs nothing), especially after either side resumes or condenses; never trust your own record of the handoff as proof of theirs.
- **Compose, don't collide.** When orchestrating on top of a domain skill that already owns a state/workpaper convention (e.g. a period-close workpaper), **defer to it** as the live coordination surface — don't stand up a duplicate generic workpaper. The `plan.md`/`workpaper.md` templates are defaults for *standalone* runs; keep `plan.md` only when it adds run-specific structure (the delegation graph/lanes/waves) the host skill doesn't already encode. **One state surface per run.**
- **Accept with a ledger.** When accepting a known benign artifact, pre-attribute its future signature at acceptance time — record exactly how it will resurface (a diff line, a delta, a discrepancy) so its reappearance classifies instantly instead of reading as a finding.
- **Verify behaviors, not just artifacts.** Artifact-level completeness sweeps miss feature drops *inside* artifacts changed on both sides. When verifying a port/merge/integration, also anchor on contracted behaviors (interfaces, route tables, promised sections) — and turn external contracts into suite assertions, so a dropped behavior fails a check rather than a consumer.
- **Route work to a session that will accept it — and keep boot surfaces neutral.** Some model/safety configurations decline adversarial-security framing (vulnerability-hunting, threat-modeling, attack-enumeration) even when the intent is defensive — the session stalls (proven 2026-07: Fable declines it; Opus accepts it framed as *architecture* — authority/principal model + guard policy + wiring). Route security-hardening units to a model that accepts them, author their charters in architecture framing (never attack framing), and keep **resume/checkpoint/boot surfaces neutral** — a fresh session can trip merely by *reading* vivid attack content, so state parked security items as terse routing pointers, never spelled-out threats.

- **Verify at the seams; grep-ground absence claims.** A unit's gate must exercise the **seam it ships into** (the artifact/service boundary), not just its own artifact — cross-artifact gaps hide exactly where single-sided tests stop, and they survive per-artifact green suites. And any claim that something is **absent or unchanged** in a pre-attributed signature/expectation must be derived from the artifact itself (a grep, a listing, a live probe), never from design prose — prose inherits its author's assumptions, and an ungrounded absence-claim can clear multiple reviewers unchallenged.

- **A verification whose pass and fail paths are not both reachable is not a verification.** Before trusting any check — a sweep, a gate, a guard clause, a clock gate — confirm it *can* fail: run a control that should trip it, or reason the failure branch is genuinely reachable (a shell pipeline's exit status is its *last* command's; a chained check that can't stop the action it guards isn't a gate). A check that prints the same verdict on clean and dirty inputs is decoration, and its "pass" is an unexamined claim. (Promoted 2026-07-29: a delegate's sweep whose failure branch was unreachable nearly reported a false wall — and would have blessed a dirty tree just as readily.)

- **Skill & doc edits follow the target's own guidelines — search for them first.** When a unit **creates or updates a skill** (core or shipped) **or runtime documentation**, the session first searches the target tree for its authoring conventions — an AUTHORING/CONTRIBUTING/style doc, the skill's own philosophy/patterns references, the release runbook, and the precedent of the last shipped change — and **conforms to what it finds**, citing the conformance in its report. Where no written guideline exists, that absence is itself a **finding to report** (a candidate for the project register), never a license to invent a private convention. Charter-side corollary: a prompt for such a unit **names the known guideline surfaces**, and the unit's gate includes the conformance check.

> These principles are the **default postures** every run inherits. A run also accrues **run-specific postures** it holds in its workpaper (Standing Postures). A compaction silently resets *both* — so `resume` re-asserts them before acting, and `checkpoint` (run before the human compacts) is where a run-specific posture proven general gets promoted up into this list. See `operations/checkpoint.md`.

## The prompt contract (every delegated prompt carries all five)
A prompt is a contract with a cold session that has none of the orchestrator's context.
1. **Load context first.** Name the docs/skills to read before any work; require the session to report what it loaded. A context-blind session silently violates conventions. For a unit that creates or updates a skill or runtime doc, name the target tree's authoring guidelines here (or require the session to search for them) — guideline conformance is part of that unit's gate.
2. **Bound the work.** Exact unit scope; the frozen base; what's already done; what to leave to siblings.
3. **Name the gate.** How the session self-verifies, and require **evidence** (command output, IDs), not assertion.
4. **Constrain writes (the lane).** The explicit, disjoint lane (worktree/branch, tables, deploy slot) + the standing rules + the **reversibility requirement** (establish rollback before a big change). ⚠ **Snapshots/backups go to `/tmp`, NEVER the tracked project or output dir** — a `.db`/`.bak` dropped in the repo dodges gitignore (e.g. `*.db` misses `foo.db.pre.bak`), gets committed, and bloats the repo. This binds the **orchestrator's own setup/pre-close snapshots** too, not just delegates'.
5. **Define the report.** A structured summary the orchestrator can re-verify against — what shipped, the evidence, anomalies — not a narrative. **Delivered on two legs: the record on the bus, and a wake on the substrate's wake channel.** The bus holds a report and announces it to nobody, so a record-only report waits unread until somebody polls. The run serializes through the orchestrator that is not looking. The wake carries one line and nothing else, `report on the bus: <ref>`, because the report is the bus record.

Every prompt also transmits the **walls-are-information rule**: the charter's words are pointers, its intent governs — if the unit hits a case/spec/assumption that breaks, the session STOPS and reports the wall with its re-derived recommendation, rather than shimming around the literal words.

## The loop
Issue the next ready unit's prompt over the bus → (spawn the delegate, or the human opens it) → session executes + reports over the bus → orchestrator **re-verifies read-only** → update the board + journal in the workpaper → release the next unit(s) (fan out independent work; serialize commitment). Converge the project's gate.

## Blocker triage (orchestrator)
Sessions never block on a human and never guess a value in — they record what they hit and return. The orchestrator then:
- **Resolves from data** whatever a read-only query/doc answers — silently, proceeds.
- **Re-charters or does it inline** when a session stalled for scope or failed its gate.
- **Routes to the human** only for genuine **crossroads** or **human-only** items — batched, plain language.
- **Halts at a hard stop** — a data-loss risk, an irreversible action without sign-off, a failed validation that can't be re-derived. Reproduce read-only, then surface the decision.

## Staying resumable
The **workpaper** is the durable coordination surface — updated every orchestrator turn (board + a one-paragraph journal). After a compaction: re-read the workpaper; the newest journal = "where we are"; the board = graph state; open human asks = what's pending. Nothing is "done" except as recorded there with re-verified evidence.
