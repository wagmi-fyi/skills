# Resume — re-bootstrap the orchestrator after a compaction (or cold re-entry)

When a persistent orchestrator session resumes — its context was **compacted**, or it's picking a dormant orchestration back up — **re-ground into the role and re-derive live state before acting. Trust nothing you didn't just re-verify.** A compaction compresses both the contract and the state; this pass restores both.

> **Did a restart take every session down, rather than one session's context?** You need `operations/recover.md` **first** — it relaunches the persistent sessions and hands each one back here.

## Why a dedicated pass
The `run` loop assumes you already hold the graph + the contract in working memory. After a compaction you don't — the summary is lossy. Skipping the re-ground is how an orchestrator drifts: it starts doing delegate work, trusts a stale report, misses a dead delegate, or deploys against a stale rev. Do this once, at re-entry, then hand off to `run`.

## The re-bootstrap checklist
1. **Re-assert your standing postures FIRST.** Read the workpaper's **Standing Postures** block — the **Effective config** (per-run overrides; honor these *over* `config.yaml` defaults) and the **Behavioral postures** — and re-affirm `method.md`'s core principles as *live directives*, not just re-read prose. A compaction silently resets postures (a run-specific deploy discipline evaporates, "drive/parallelize" fades to over-caution); re-asserting them here is what stops the drift. A posture proven general gets promoted to a default via `checkpoint`; until then it lives in the workpaper and you re-assert it from there.
2. **Re-read the contract — fully, not the SKILL.md summary.** Re-read `reference/{human-in-the-loop,method,session-bus,substrate,git,decision-briefs}.md`, the runbook for this run's substrate, and the operation you drive (`run`). This restores the rules a compaction blurs: the orchestrator *mutates nothing / never builds or deploys itself*; pause only on *design crossroads / human-only-irreversible*; *verify-don't-trust*; *parallelize cognition, serialize commitment*; the 5-part prompt contract; the commitment-lease discipline.
3. **Load `config.yaml`** — `output_dir`, `bus_dir`, `spawn_mode`, `delegate_model` — then apply the workpaper's **Effective config** deltas on top. Those overrides are the run's *real* settings; the bare `config.yaml` values are only the baseline.
4. **Read the resume surface.** The workpaper's STATUS box + **newest journal entry = "where we are"** + `plan.md` (graph/gates). If the project composes on a host skill's workpaper, that's the surface — don't stand up a duplicate.
5. **Re-register your handle before you read any board.** A resume keeps the registration and gives the session a new process, so the process the bus recorded is dead while you are alive: `bus handles` prints your own handle `gone`, and a peer looking you up aims a wake at a session that ended. Re-register in the form your runbook gives, carrying your current listing name as the address. Do it here, before the survey below reads a board that has your own row wrong.
6. **Survey the shared bus (read-only)** — `bus handles` + `bus locks`. **A commitment lease may have EXPIRED while you were idle — that's benign; never hold one across idle time, re-take it at the next commitment.** Read your own inbox for anything that landed. Use `bus inbox <you>`: `bus read` shows the same messages and leaves the cursor where it was, so a board that still says unread after a `read` is telling the truth.
7. **Take each in-flight delegate's disposition from the board, and its work from its lane.** Read the board the way this run's runbook says to, including its warning about which signals lie (`substrate.md`). Classify every in-flight unit as working, blocked, finished or gone, and **handle blocked first** — it is parked on something only you can give it. A session that is gone is **not lost work**: the unit's output lives in its commits, so check the lane (branch, HEAD sha, worktree clean?) and decide between re-chartering against that base or finishing inline.

   **This pass is not only a re-entry ritual.** `run.md` makes the same read the every-beat discipline, because a finished delegate and a blocked one can look identical and neither one announces itself.

   **Addressing:** the bus handle is not the agent name. Take the address from the listing verbatim, and append the bracketed ref only when the listing shows the name twice or an error asks you to. A guessed address fails silently.

   **Also sweep the board for handles with no live session** — a worker registered on the bus and absent from the listing is a closed unit or a dead one; reconcile it against the workpaper board rather than leaving it to read as in-flight.
8. **Confirm your own reach.** Your handle is registered from step 5. State your wake: whichever mechanism the runbook offers, armed and proven, or manual board checks. **Do not restart a wake mechanism your substrate has no delivery path for** — an undeliverable poke is not a misconfigured one, and no amount of debugging fixes it. Unread counts on `bus handles` are the truth in every configuration.
9. **Re-derive load-bearing live state read-only (trust nothing).** The workpaper records what was true *when written*. Before any decision, re-verify the facts it depends on from source-of-truth — deploy rev + image tags, branch/commit shas, DB/PVC state, credential validity, whatever your next step keys on. This is where a stale rev, a reverted tag, or a dead credential gets caught.
10. **Record the resume** in the workpaper (re-grounded; postures re-asserted; what you re-verified; each delegate's status; the lease state; what's next) — so the *next* resume is clean.
11. **Re-enter `run`.** Re-verify any pending delegate reports read-only before trusting them, fan out ready work (parallelize freely), serialize commitments under the lease.

## Durable gotchas (learned the hard way)
- **A compaction resets postures, not just state.** Standing directives silently revert to skill defaults after a compaction. Re-assert the workpaper's Standing Postures block *first* — that's why `checkpoint` pins it before you compact.
- **A session that ended is not a unit that failed.** The work is in the commit. Check the lane before you conclude anything, and never re-charter a fresh delegate over a lane you haven't read.
- **The address comes from the board, never from the bus.** Where a substrate keeps two namespaces, they drift by design: the bus knows the handle you chose, the host knows the session's own name. Take the address verbatim from wherever the runbook says it lives.
- **`bus gc --days 0` retires live handles, including yours.** Idle is always ≥ 0, so every fully-read handle qualifies. The 14-day default is the protection. It is reversible (archived to `processed/`, still in `log.jsonl`), and re-registering is the fix.
- **An idle lease expires — and that's correct.** Re-take at the commitment point; don't hold one across idle time, and don't panic that it lapsed.
- **Parallelize aggressively.** Fan out at the *unit and the phase* level; only the commitment step (deploy / merge-to-mainline / publish) serializes. Post-compaction it's easy to over-serialize out of caution — resist it.
- **Verify-don't-trust survives compaction too.** Re-derive every returning report's gate from source, never the summary — most of all right after a resume, when your memory of "what's already done" is the lossy part.
- **The workpaper is the handoff.** Update it every turn during the run so that *this* pass has something faithful to resume from. A compaction is survivable exactly to the degree the newest journal entry is a real handoff.
